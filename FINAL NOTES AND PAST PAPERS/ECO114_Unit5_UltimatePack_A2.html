2. Institutions, Power & Pirate Economics
0% complete
Institutions→Power→ Feasible choices→Allocation→ Efficiency + Fairness judgement

Institution (Textbook definition)

A set of laws and informal rules that regulate social interactions among people, and between people and the biosphere. Sometimes also called "the rules of the game". They determine who can do what, when they can do it, and what the pay-offs are.

Power (Two Forms)

Structural power: The value of your next best alternative (reservation option). Better outside option = more structural power = can walk away from bad deals.

Bargaining power: Ability to set the terms of exchange, make take-it-or-leave-it offers, or impose costs on the other party.

The Royal Rover: Pirate Economics

Captain Bartholomew Roberts' pirate ship had a written constitution. This shows that even illegal enterprises need institutions to coordinate behaviour and divide gains. The Royal Rover's articles were the rules of the game for that ship's economy.

Why can the same technology produce very different outcomes?
Because institutions change who has power, what options are feasible, and how any surplus is divided. The production possibilities curve is the same in all Angela/Bruno cases — the feasible frontier does not change. But the institution (rules of the game) determines where on or inside that frontier the outcome lies, and who receives each share.
How do institutions affect the ultimatum game?
In the standard ultimatum game, the Proposer has take-it-or-leave-it bargaining power. If there are two Responders, each is less certain the other will hold out, so the Proposer can offer less. If the rules gave the Proposer a fallback of zero and the Responder a fallback of 40%, the Responder would reject any offer below 40%. The institution (rules) directly changes what each player can get.

4. Angela/Bruno Model Foundations
0% complete

Angela — What she wants

Angela values grain (income/food) and free time. Her indifference curves are downward sloping: she is willing to give up some grain for more free time. Higher indifference curves = higher utility.

Key assumption (quasi-linear preferences): Angela's MRS depends only on her free time, NOT on how much grain she has. This means her indifference curves are vertical shifts of each other.

Bruno — What he wants

Bruno cares only about how much grain he receives. He does not farm; he owns the land. He does not care how much free time Angela has — only how much grain he gets. His indifference curves are horizontal lines.

His reservation option = 0 bushels (if Angela refuses/escapes). So any grain he receives is a positive economic rent.

Reading the table: At h=8 hours, Angela produces 46 bushels. Free time = 24−8 = 16 hours. This 8-hour / 46-bushel / 16-hour-free-time point appears in EVERY case as the optimum (because MRS=MRT always occurs at 16 hours free time in this model).

MRS — Marginal Rate of Substitution

The slope of Angela's indifference curve (at any point). It represents: how many bushels of grain Angela is willing to sacrifice for one extra hour of free time.

MRS = how much grain she values free time (willingness to trade)

MRT — Marginal Rate of Transformation

The slope of the feasible frontier (at any point). It represents: how much grain Angela must sacrifice to gain one more hour of free time (= the marginal product of an hour of work).

MRT = opportunity cost of free time in terms of grain foregone

The feasible frontier (from the production function) and Angela's indifference curves. The optimum is at A (16 hours free, 46 bushels) where the curves are tangent: MRS = MRT.

5. Independent Farmer — Baseline
0% complete

In the baseline case, Angela owns the land herself. There is no Bruno. She chooses her hours to maximise her utility, keeping all the grain she produces. This is a one-person constrained choice problem.

Exam Wording (use this): "As an independent farmer, Angela chooses the combination of grain and free time that maximises her utility subject to the feasible frontier. Why does Angela not choose to have 24 hours of free time and produce 0 grain?
Because she needs grain to survive and consume. A point with 24 hours free and 0 grain is on the feasible frontier but gives her lower utility than point A where she has some of both goods. Her indifference curves show she values both grain and free time.
Case 1
6. Forced Labour — Coercion
0% complete

Bruno owns the land AND can coerce Angela physically. The government enforces his right to use force. Angela's only option is to comply or attempt to escape/revolt (risky and costly). Her reservation indifference curve IC1 represents her minimum acceptable utility under threat of force.

Reservation Indifference Curve IC1

Each point on IC1 gives Angela exactly the utility she would get from disobeying Bruno and risking escape or revolt. Bruno must keep Angela on or above IC1 to prevent resistance. Below IC1 = Angela revolts.

Bruno's Feasible Set

The lens-shaped region between the feasible frontier (above) and IC1 (below). All allocations here are feasible AND will be complied with. Bruno maximises his grain within this region.

How Bruno Maximises — The Logic

Bruno keeps Angela exactly on IC1 (giving her no economic rent). The grain he captures is the vertical distance between the feasible frontier and IC1. He maximises this vertical distance by choosing where:

Optimal condition for Bruno under coercion:
MRT (slope of feasible frontier) = MRS (slope of IC1 at that point)

// To the left (Angela works more): MRS > MRT → Bruno should give her MORE free time
// To the right (Angela works less): MRS < MRT → Bruno should give her LESS free time
// Optimum is at 16 hours free time (same as Angela's own choice!)

Bruno's rent = Total grain − Angela's survival grain
= 46 − 15 = 31 bushels

Why is Case 1 Pareto efficient? Consider any change from allocation D. If Angela gets more grain, Bruno is worse off. If hours change, Bruno's maximum grain decreases. If Angela is pushed below IC1, she revolts and both get nothing. Therefore no change can make one person better off without making another worse off → Pareto efficient. This is the most counterintuitive result in Unit 5 — learn it word-for-word.
Exam Trap: "If Angela survives, the outcome must be fair." FALSE. Survival only means Bruno gave her IC1 — it says nothing about fairness, equality, or consent. An allocation can be efficient AND deeply unfair.
Why does Angela work the same hours in Case 1 as in the baseline?
Because Angela's preferences (quasi-linear) mean MRS at any level of free time is the same regardless of how much grain she has. So MRS = MRT is always satisfied at 16 hours free time (8 hours work), regardless of how grain is distributed. Bruno maximises his share by choosing 16 hours free time — exactly the same as Angela would choose for herself.
Case 2
7. Take-It-or-Leave-It Contract
0% complete

In Case 2, Bruno owns the land but cannot use physical force. The government protects Angela from coercion and enforces contracts. Angela can refuse and seek work elsewhere — her reservation option is much better than in Case 1. But Bruno still has bargaining power: he makes the take-it-or-leave-it offer.

Government protects Angela→ Angela can refuse→ IC2 is above IC1→ Bruno must offer more→ Bruno's rent falls (31 → 23)

Employment Contract

Bruno specifies hours (8) and wage (23 bushels). Angela accepts if wage ≥ IC2. Bruno makes a take-it-or-leave-it offer at minimum acceptable wage, capturing all joint surplus.

Condition: Bruno offers a contract such that Angela is exactly on IC2 (reservation utility). MRS on IC2 = MRT at optimum → same 16 hours free time, 8 hours work.

Tenancy Contract

Bruno specifies a land rent of 23 bushels. Angela is free to choose her own hours. She solves her own optimum with consumption = output − 23. She ends up choosing 8 hours work (same as before) and consuming 23 bushels.

The outcome is identical — Bruno gets the same 23 bushels whether he sets hours directly (employment) or sets a land rent (tenancy).

Key insight on joint surplus: The joint surplus is maximised at MRS = MRT → 23 bushels (= 46 total − 23 for Angela's IC2). Since Bruno makes the take-it-or-leave-it offer, he captures the entire joint surplus as rent. Angela gets exactly her reservation utility, zero rent. Voluntary exchange does not mean equal surplus sharing.
Trap: "Angela must be better off in Case 2 than Case 1." TRUE for Angela (15 → 23 bushels). FALSE for Bruno (31 → 23). The Pareto criterion cannot rank Case 1 vs Case 2: Angela prefers Case 2, Bruno prefers Case 1. Neither Pareto dominates the other.
Why does the tenancy contract produce the same outcome as the employment contract?
Because Angela's quasi-linear preferences mean that when she faces a consumption frontier of (output − 23), she still chooses 8 hours work to satisfy MRS = MRT on her consumption frontier. This is exactly point L: 16 hours free, 23 bushels grain consumed, 23 bushels to Bruno as rent. Bruno correctly anticipates this and sets the land rent at 23.
Case 3
8. Bargaining in a Democracy
0% complete

In Case 3, Angela and other farmers can vote. They elect a government that passes legislation: a maximum working week and minimum wage equal to what Angela would earn in Case 2. Now Angela has both structural power (better reservation option) and some bargaining power (she and Bruno can negotiate, not just accept/reject).

Step 1: Legal Minimum

The law sets: maximum 4.5 hours work, minimum wage = 23 bushels (same as Case 2). This moves Angela to a point above IC2 — she gets the same 23 bushels for fewer hours work (19.5 hours free instead of 16). Angela is better off than in Case 2.

Step 2: Further Bargaining

At the legal minimum, MRS ≠ MRT. Both Angela and Bruno can gain by renegotiating: Angela works slightly more, the extra grain is shared. This is a Pareto improvement over the legal minimum.

Why is the legal minimum NOT automatically Pareto efficient? Because it restricts work to 4.5 hours, not 8 hours. At 4.5 hours work (19.5 hours free), MRT > MRS: the grain that could be produced by one extra hour of work exceeds the grain Angela would need to compensate her for losing that free time. So there is a potential mutual gain from increasing hours and sharing the extra grain. The parties are not at the MRS = MRT point — a Pareto improvement is still possible.

How can both Bruno and Angela be better off after bargaining vs the legal minimum?
At the legal minimum, MRT > MRS. This means one extra hour of Angela's work produces more grain than she needs to be compensated (to stay on the same IC). If she works more and shares the extra grain, Bruno gets more grain and Angela is no worse off (or slightly better off too). Both gain relative to the legal minimum. This is a Pareto improvement. It continues until MRS = MRT, where no further mutual gain is possible.

9. Pareto Efficiency & Fairness — The Core Tension
0% complete
The critical distinction Unit 5 tests every year: An allocation can be Pareto efficient and deeply unfair at the same time. Efficiency asks whether any mutual gain is still possible. Fairness asks whether the distribution is just, equal, or legitimate.

Pareto Efficiency

An allocation is Pareto efficient if there is NO feasible alternative in which at least one person is better off and nobody is worse off.

  • Does NOT require equality
  • Does NOT require consent or fairness
  • A brutal dictatorship can be Pareto efficient
  • Case 1 (forced labour) IS Pareto efficient

Fairness (Two Types)

Substantive: Judged by what people get — income, wellbeing, freedom. Equal pay for equal work. Different criteria lead to different conclusions.

Procedural: Judged by how the outcome came about — free choice, equal opportunity, legitimacy, no force. Case 1 fails on procedural grounds even if both survive.

Rawls' Veil of Ignorance

The philosopher John Rawls argued for evaluating institutions from behind a "veil of ignorance" — not knowing what position you would occupy in society (rich/poor, Angela/Bruno). From this impartial position, you evaluate the rules of the game. Would you accept the rules of forced labour if you did not know whether you would be Angela or Bruno?

High-yield sentence (copy in exam answers): "An allocation can be Pareto efficient and still be unfair, because Pareto efficiency only asks whether any mutual gains remain, not whether the distribution of power, opportunity, or surplus is just."

11. Lorenz Curve & Gini Coefficient

Gini = Area A ÷ (Area A + Area B under the equality line). A curve bowing further from the equality line = higher Gini = more inequality.

Can two countries have the same Gini but very different living standards?
Yes. A rich country and a poor country can have identical Gini values (e.g., 0.35) but the rich country's poorest citizens may be absolutely wealthier than the poor country's richest citizens. Gini measures relative inequality (how the pie is shared), not the absolute size of the pie.

12. Redistribution & Policy Applications
0% complete

Redistribution via Income

Taxes and transfers: progressive income tax + transfers (welfare, grants) reduce market income inequality. Market income Gini > Disposable income Gini after redistribution.

Redistribution via Rights

Labour laws, voting rights, property rights: change bargaining power before income is even distributed. Case 3 shows how democratic rights shift the balance without explicit tax transfers.

Redistribution via Policy Design

Policies can affect both the size of the surplus AND who gets it. Sometimes removing a power imbalance that prevents efficient bargaining can simultaneously improve fairness and efficiency.

Longer-question warning: Avoid vague moral language like "this is unfair" or "this is bad for society." Convert fairness into economics: who has power, what is the reservation option, who receives surplus, was the process voluntary, are property rights legitimate?

Q1. In Case 1, Angela receives no economic rent. The outcome is Pareto efficient.
TRUE. Angela is on IC1 with zero rent above her reservation option. The outcome IS Pareto efficient — any change makes at least one party worse off. Case 1 shows that efficiency and fairness are completely different criteria.
Q2. In Case 2 (take-it-or-leave-it), the exchange is voluntary, therefore both parties receive an equal share of the joint surplus.
FALSE. Voluntariness does not imply equal surplus sharing. Since Bruno makes the take-it-or-leave-it offer, he captures the entire joint surplus (23 bushels in the example). Angela receives zero economic rent — she is exactly at her reservation utility.
Q3. Angela's MRS at 16 hours of free time is the same on every indifference curve.
TRUE. This is the quasi-linear preference assumption. The slope of Angela's ICs at any given free-time level is the same regardless of how much grain she has. This is why Angela always works 8 hours at the optimum, regardless of which case we are in.
Q4. The Lorenz curve shows how much output is produced at different levels of employment.
FALSE. The Lorenz curve shows the distribution of income or wealth, NOT production levels. It plots cumulative income share against cumulative population share. Do not confuse it with a production function.
Q5. An allocation that is Pareto efficient must also be substantively fair.
FALSE. This is THE most important Unit 5 trap. Case 1 (forced labour, Angela gets 15, Bruno gets 31) is Pareto efficient but substantively extremely unfair. Pareto efficiency only says no mutual gains remain — it says nothing about equality or justice.
Q6. Under a tenancy contract in Case 2, Angela freely chooses how many hours to work and ends up working 8 hours.
TRUE. With a land rent of 23 bushels, Angela's consumption frontier is (output − 23). She maximises utility on this frontier, choosing 8 hours work (16 hours free) — exactly where MRS = MRT for her consumption frontier. This is point L.
Q7. In Case 1, Bruno would maximise his grain by forcing Angela to work 24 hours.
FALSE. Bruno faces a trade-off. More work produces more total grain but Angela requires more grain to survive at higher work intensities (biological survival constraint rises steeply). Bruno's optimal is at 16 hours free time / 8 hours work — where the slope of the feasible frontier equals the slope of IC1.
Q8. The feasible frontier is the mirror image of the production function, with free time on the horizontal axis instead of hours worked.
TRUE. Since free time = 24 − hours worked, the feasible frontier plots the same grain values but against free time (24−h). It is a horizontal reflection of the production function.
Q9. A Gini coefficient of 0 means everyone is equally poor.
FALSE. Gini = 0 means perfect equality of distribution — everyone has the same share of income or wealth. It says nothing about the absolute level (whether people are rich or poor). A society of equally wealthy millionaires has Gini = 0.
Q10. In Case 3, the initial legal minimum (maximum hours law) is Pareto efficient.
FALSE. The legal minimum restricts hours to fewer than 8. At this point MRT > MRS — the extra grain from one more hour of work exceeds what Angela would need to compensate for her lost free time. A Pareto improvement (both better off through further bargaining) is still possible. The legal minimum is NOT Pareto efficient.
Q11. Structural power depends on the quality of a person's next best alternative to the current interaction.
TRUE. This is exactly the definition. Angela's structural power is higher in Case 2 than Case 1 because her alternative option (other employment) is better than her Case 1 alternative (escape/revolt). Better outside options → more structural power → higher reservation indifference curve.
Q12. The joint surplus is maximised at the same point regardless of which case we are in (Case 1, 2, or 3).
TRUE (with nuance). Because Angela has quasi-linear preferences, MRS = MRT always occurs at 16 hours free time in this model, regardless of which IC we are constrained to. The total grain produced is always 46 bushels at the joint-surplus-maximising point. However, what changes between cases is Angela's reservation option, which changes how the joint surplus is split.
Q13. Procedural fairness asks whether the outcome (allocation) is equal or just.
FALSE. Procedural fairness asks whether the process by which the outcome was reached was fair — was it voluntary? Did people have equal opportunity? Was there coercion or fraud? Substantive fairness asks about the outcome characteristics (how equal, how much wellbeing).
Q14. Under a take-it-or-leave-it contract, Angela's working hours are necessarily the same as under forced labour.
TRUE (in this model). Because of quasi-linear preferences, MRS at any free-time level is the same on every IC. Bruno maximises his grain at MRS = MRT in both cases, which is 16 hours free (8 hours work). The hours do not change — only the distribution of grain changes between cases.
Q15. Market income Gini is always lower than disposable income Gini.
FALSE — it is the other way around. Market income Gini is usually HIGHER (more unequal) than disposable income Gini, because taxes (progressive) and transfers (to low-income earners) reduce inequality. Disposable income is what remains after redistribution, making it more equal.
Q16. Bargaining power and structural power are the same thing.
FALSE. Structural power is determined by your next best alternative (the value of your outside option). Bargaining power is the ability to set terms, make take-it-or-leave-it offers, or impose costs. They are related but distinct. Angela has more structural power in Case 2 (better outside option) but Bruno retains all bargaining power (he makes the offer).
Q17. Rawls' veil of ignorance is used to evaluate the Pareto efficiency of an allocation.
FALSE. The veil of ignorance is used to evaluate fairness. Rawls argues that to assess whether a set of rules or institutions is just, we should evaluate it as if we do not know which position in society we will occupy. It is a thought experiment for procedural justice, not an efficiency criterion.
Q18. In the Angela/Bruno model, Bruno's indifference curves are horizontal lines.
TRUE. Bruno only cares about how much grain he receives; he is indifferent to Angela's free time. A horizontal IC means: for any given amount of grain, all levels of Angela's free time give Bruno the same utility. Higher horizontal lines = more grain = higher utility for Bruno.
Q19. If Angela's biological survival constraint shifts upward, Bruno will be able to extract more grain from Angela.
FALSE. If the biological survival constraint shifts upward (Angela needs more grain to survive at each work level), Angela's reservation IC effectively rises. Bruno must give Angela more grain to keep her compliant. Bruno's extracted rent DECREASES, not increases.
Q20. An employment contract and a tenancy contract always produce the same outcome for Angela and Bruno.
TRUE (in this model). Under the tenancy contract (rent = 23 bushels), Angela freely chooses 8 hours work to maximise her utility. This gives the same outcome as the employment contract (8 hours specified, wage 23 bushels). Bruno correctly anticipates this when setting the land rent. The key insight: the form of the contract does not matter in this model — both yield allocation L.

Q1: Coercion vs voluntary exchange (from tutorial)

Given: total grain at 8h work = 8 bushels, at 11h work = 10 bushels.

Answer: Can Bruno be just as well off under voluntary exchange as under coercion?
Under coercion, Bruno's best outcome keeps Angela on IC1. Under voluntary exchange, Angela's IC2 is higher (better outside option). Bruno must offer more to Angela under voluntary exchange. So NO — Bruno cannot be just as well off under voluntary exchange. His maximum rent is strictly lower when Angela has the right to refuse.

Note: In the specific textbook example, Bruno gets 31 under coercion and only 23 under voluntary exchange — the same proportion applies here.

Q3: Jim (farmer) and Dwight (landowner)

Why does the biological survival constraint get steeper as Jim works more?
More work requires more energy expenditure. As Jim works more hours, his body burns more calories, so he needs more food (beet) to survive. The additional beet required rises with work intensity — the survival constraint becomes steeper. This mirrors Angela's survival constraint: it rises faster than the feasible frontier at high work levels.
What happens to (a) biological survival constraint and (b) feasible frontier if a more nutritious beet is available?
(a) Biological survival constraint: Shifts downward — more nutritious beet means Jim needs fewer kilograms to survive at each work level, so the minimum required beet for each work level falls.

(b) Feasible frontier: Shifts upward — more nutritious beet may also mean better agricultural output (or at minimum, the same output with different properties). If "more nutritious" means higher market value or same production, frontier shifts out. If Jim cares about nutrition not quantity, the effective value of each bushel rises.

Q5: Peter Parker (pomegranates) and Tony Stark

Q5.1: Which combinations are biologically infeasible?
Any combination that lies below the biological survival constraint is biologically infeasible — it does not give Peter enough pomegranates to maintain health and work. Points below the BSC line in the diagram are infeasible because Peter would starve or be unable to work. Points above the feasible frontier are technologically infeasible because they require more output than is producible.
Q5.2: If Tony offers 3 tons, how many hours does Peter work?
Peter maximises his utility subject to his consumption = output − 3 tons (he keeps output minus the wage payment to Tony). He finds the point where his IC is tangent to this shifted feasible frontier. The answer depends on where MRS = MRT on his consumption frontier — without specific curve data, the answer is: Peter works the hours that maximise his utility given the wage. If 3 tons is below his IC2 (reservation utility), he rejects the offer.
Q5.3: At point A, Tony offers 5 tons for 8 hours — should Peter accept?
Peter should accept if and only if the combination (8 hours work, 5 tons of pomegranates) lies on or above his reservation indifference curve IC. If point A is on or above IC, he is at least as well off as his next best alternative, so he accepts. If it is below IC, he rejects. In the context of the model, Tony making a take-it-or-leave-it offer would set the wage at exactly the IC level — so the wage of 5 tons may well be at or just above reservation utility.

The opportunity cost of one hour of free time — how much grain must be sacrificed. Slope of the feasible frontier = marginal product of labour.

NO. At fewer than 8 hours work, MRT > MRS. A Pareto improvement is still available through further bargaining.
Define: economic rent
The benefit from a chosen action above the benefit of the next best alternative (reservation option). Angela has zero rent in Cases 1 and 2 (she's on her reservation IC).
Define: structural power
Power from having a better fallback option — ability to walk away from a bad deal. Better outside option = more structural power.
Define: joint surplus
Sum of economic rents of all parties — total gain above all parties' reservation options. In Case 2: 46 − 23 = 23 bushels above IC2.
Difference between IC1 and IC2?
IC1 = Angela's reservation under coercion (escape/revolt). IC2 = Angela's reservation under voluntary exchange (alternative employment). IC2 is above IC1 — better outside option.
Substantive vs Procedural fairness?
Substantive: judged by the outcome (how equal is it?). Procedural: judged by the process (was it voluntary? legitimate? equal opportunity?).
Lorenz curve: what does a point (x%, y%) mean?
The bottom x% of the population earns y% of total income. The further this is below the 45° line, the more unequal.
Gini = 0 means what?
Perfect equality — every % of population earns the same % of income. The Lorenz curve coincides with the 45° line. NOT necessarily rich or poor.
AP formula and Angela example
AP = Y/h. At h=8, Y=46: AP = 46/8 = 5.75 bushels per hour.
MP formula (interval) and Angela example

What is quasi-linear preference (one implication)?
Angela's MRS at any given level of free time is the same on every indifference curve. ICs are vertical shifts of each other. Result: optimal hours are always 8 regardless of which case.
What is the "lens-shaped region" in Case 1?
The region between the feasible frontier (top boundary) and IC1 (bottom boundary). These are all allocations Bruno can enforce — Angela won't revolt and production is feasible.
Why does the tenancy contract give the same outcome as employment?
Angela with consumption = output − 23 maximises at 8h work (where MRS = MRT on her consumption frontier). Bruno sets rent = 23 to capture this. Outcome = allocation L in both cases.
Define: Pareto improvement
A change that makes at least one person better off without making anyone else worse off. If ANY person is made worse off, it is NOT a Pareto improvement.

8 hours → 46 bushels. 12 hours → 54 bushels. (From production function table: critical numbers for exam calculations.)
What do pirate articles illustrate in Unit 5?
Even illegal/violent organisations need institutions (rules of the game) to coordinate behaviour and share gains. Rules determine who has power and what each person gets.
Why does MRT increase as free time increases?
Because of diminishing marginal product. When Angela already has a lot of free time, each extra hour of free time costs her more grain (she was at a steeper part of the production function). MRT = |slope of feasible frontier|, which rises as we move right.
What happens to Bruno's rent if Angela's alternative option improves (IC2 shifts up)?
Bruno's rent falls. A higher IC2 means Bruno must offer Angela more grain to get her to accept. The gap between the feasible frontier and IC2 (= joint surplus Bruno can capture) shrinks.
Can Case 2 Pareto dominate Case 1?
NO. In Case 2, Angela is better off (15→23 bushels) but Bruno is worse off (31→23 bushels). Neither case Pareto dominates the other. The Pareto criterion cannot rank them.
What is the veil of ignorance?
Rawls' thought experiment: evaluate the rules of a society as if you did not know which position you would occupy (Angela or Bruno, rich or poor). Enables impartial fairness judgements.
Name 3 dimensions institutions can affect (from Unit 5)
(1) Who has power (structural and bargaining). (2) Total pay-offs available (size of joint surplus). (3) How the total is divided (distribution of surplus between parties).
If MRS > MRT at a point, what should happen?
Angela should take MORE free time. The grain she is willing to sacrifice for one extra hour of free time exceeds what she would actually have to give up (MRT). Moving right increases her utility.
If MRT > MRS at a point, what should happen?
Angela should take LESS free time (work more). Each hour of work produces more grain than Angela needs to compensate for the lost free time. Moving left increases her utility.
What is market income?
Income BEFORE taxes and government transfers. Usually more unequal. Market income Gini > Disposable income Gini in countries with progressive tax systems.
A2 scope: which units are tested?
Units 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, and 10. Unit 5 = micro institutions model (Angela/Bruno). NOT Unit 5 macro (inflation/unemployment — that is a different Unit 5).
Why does Angela work the same hours in Cases 1, 2, and the baseline?
Quasi-linear preferences: MRS at any free-time level is the same across all ICs. So MRS = MRT is always satisfied at 16 hours free time / 8h work, regardless of which IC or constraint is binding.
What makes an exchange "procedurally fair"?
The process: voluntary exchange (no coercion), equal opportunity, legitimate property rights, non-discrimination, freedom to refuse. NOT just whether the outcome is equal.
Define: reservation indifference curve
A curve showing combinations as good as the reservation option. Angela stays on or above this curve; Bruno must ensure she reaches it (otherwise she refuses/revolts). In Case 1 = IC1; in Case 2 = IC2.
What happens to Angela's rent in Case 3 vs Case 2?
In Case 3, Angela gains some economic rent (positive) because the democratic legislation raises her reservation option AND she can now bargain. In Case 2 she has zero rent.
Production function: what does "concave" mean?
Diminishing returns: each additional hour of work adds less output than the previous hour (because land is fixed). The production function flattens as hours increase. This makes the feasible frontier bow outward.
What is the Pareto efficiency curve in Angela/Bruno?
A vertical line at 16 hours free time (8 hours work) — because quasi-linear preferences mean all Pareto-efficient allocations have the same hours. Only the grain distribution between Angela and Bruno varies along this line.
