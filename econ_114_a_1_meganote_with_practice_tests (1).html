2) Ultra-fast formula sheet

GDP growth % = ((new GDP - old GDP) / old GDP) × 100
GDP per capita = GDP / population
Relative price of X in terms of Y = Px / Py
Technology cost = wL + rK
At consumer optimum: MRS = relative price
Innovation rent = old cost - new cost (if output price stays the same)
Comparative advantage = lower opportunity cost
Nash equilibrium = no player wants to deviate unilaterally

3) Section A MegaNote — what to know cold

Malthusian trap

  • Land is effectively fixed.
  • As population rises, the average product of labour falls.
  • If income rises above subsistence, population tends to increase.
  • That larger population then pushes income per person back down.
  • So temporary productivity gains do not permanently raise living standards in the trap.

Always remember

  • MRS = slope of indifference curve
  • MRT = slope of feasible frontier / budget line trade-off in production context
  • Nash equilibrium ≠ always socially best
  • Pareto efficient = no one can be made better off without making someone else worse off

Section A answer style

Do not answer based on vibe. Ask: Is every word correct? A statement can start well and still be false because of one wrong causal step or one swapped definition.

4) Section B MegaNote — repeated question types

Comparative advantage

Work out the opportunity cost for each side. The one with the lower opportunity cost has the comparative advantage.

Opportunity cost of 1 X = amount of Y given up

Technology choice

Eliminate dominated techniques first. Then compare total costs using input prices.

Cost = wL + rK

Budget line + MRS

If the consumer is at the best affordable point, then the indifference curve is tangent to the budget line.

MRS = Px / Py (using magnitudes)

Income + substitution

Price change → one good becomes relatively cheaper or more expensive → substitution effect. Real purchasing power also changes → income effect.

Game theory

Check best responses row by row and column by column. If one strategy is always best no matter what the other does, it is dominant.

GDP / GDP per capita

A fall in GDP does not automatically mean everyone is worse off. Population changes can make GDP per capita move differently from total GDP.

5) Section C MegaNote — clean exam answers

Template 1: Explain the Malthusian trap

In the Malthusian model, land is effectively fixed, so when population increases, more labour is applied to the same land. This causes the average product of labour to fall. If wages or income rise above subsistence, population increases. That increase in population pushes output per worker back down, so living standards return toward subsistence. Therefore, productivity gains do not permanently raise living standards while the economy remains in the Malthusian trap.

Template 2: Comparative advantage

First calculate the opportunity cost of producing each good for each person/country. The person or country with the lower opportunity cost of producing a good has the comparative advantage in that good. Therefore, specialisation should follow comparative advantage, not absolute advantage.

Template 3: Technology choice + innovation rent

The firm should choose the non-dominated technology with the lowest total cost. Total cost is calculated as wage times labour plus the price of capital times capital. Innovation rent is the extra profit earned from using the new lower-cost technology before rivals adopt it. If output price stays the same, the innovation rent is equal to the old cost minus the new cost.

Template 4: Income and substitution effects

The substitution effect is the change in consumption caused by a good becoming relatively cheaper or more expensive. The income effect is the change caused by the change in real purchasing power. For a normal good, a fall in price usually increases consumption through both effects. In a labour-leisure model, a wage increase causes a substitution effect toward less free time and more work, but the income effect may increase free time if leisure is a normal good.

Template 5: Nash equilibrium / social dilemma

A Nash equilibrium is an outcome where neither player wants to deviate unilaterally. A social dilemma occurs when the Nash equilibrium is Pareto inefficient, meaning both players could be better off at another feasible outcome, but self-interested behaviour prevents them from reaching it.

Template 6: Pareto efficiency in bargaining / farm diagrams

An allocation is Pareto efficient if no further mutually beneficial reallocation is possible. Where MRS equals MRT, the person’s willingness to trade matches the economy’s trade-off, so the gains from trade have been exhausted. Therefore, no Pareto improvement from that point is possible.

6) Common traps that cost easy marks

MRS vs MRT: MRS is about preferences. MRT is about trade-offs in production / feasible choices.
Absolute vs comparative advantage: being better at both goods does not remove comparative advantage.
GDP vs wellbeing: GDP is useful, but does not measure everything that matters.
Nash equilibrium: not automatically fair, efficient, or socially best.
Innovation rent: it is a gain from lower cost, not just from inventing something cool.
Inferior good: income effect may go the opposite way from the substitution effect.

7) Extra past-paper clarifications

Graphs in A1

Across the past A1 papers, students were generally not asked to draw full graphs from scratch. A1 mainly expects you to interpret provided graphs, explain movements between points, explain shifts, identify feasible vs unfeasible bundles, and derive equations for budget lines or isocost lines.

Translation for revision: know how to read graphs fluently, not just memorise how to sketch them.

How much Adam Smith matters

You do not need a big biography. What matters is linking Adam Smith to:

  • specialisation
  • division of labour
  • market coordination / spontaneous order

You are much more likely to get a conceptual question than a date-based history question.

Negative marking

  • Section A: usually +1 for correct, −1/2 for incorrect
  • Section B: usually +2 for correct, −1/4 for incorrect

This means random guessing can hurt you, especially in Section A.

Important pattern

Past papers care far more about whether you can explain why a model works than whether you can recall dates, inventors, or trivia. Learn the logic, not just the labels.

Practice Test 1 — Malthus + Tech + GDP

1. In the Malthusian trap, a productivity increase permanently raises living standards.

False. Higher income leads to population growth, which pushes income per person back down toward subsistence.

2. Which conditions are necessary for an economy to remain in the Malthusian trap?

The trap needs diminishing average product of labour plus a population response to higher income.

3. A new technology lowers production cost while output price stays the same. The firm’s extra gain is called:

Innovation rent is the extra profit from adopting a lower-cost technology before others do.

4. GDP per capita can rise even when total GDP falls if:

GDP per capita depends on both GDP and population.

5. Britain escaped the Malthusian trap in the Allen model mainly because:

Cheap coal + expensive labour gave firms an incentive to adopt energy-intensive, labour-saving technology.
Practice Test 2 — Consumer theory

1. At the best affordable consumption point, the consumer usually chooses the bundle where:

At the optimum, the slope of the indifference curve equals the slope of the budget line.

2. If the price of a normal good falls, the substitution effect on that good is:

When a good becomes relatively cheaper, consumers substitute toward it.

3. The slope of the budget line shows:

Its magnitude is the opportunity cost of one good in terms of the other.

4. If leisure is a normal good and wages rise, the substitution effect on free time is:

A higher wage makes free time more expensive, so the substitution effect reduces free time.

5. For an inferior good, the income effect after a fall in real income is usually:

When real income falls, consumers often buy more of an inferior good.

11) Last-minute rescue answers

If you forget Malthus

Fixed land + more people → lower output per person. Higher income → more population. Population growth cancels the income gain.

If you forget innovation rent

It is just the cost saving from the new technology, as long as output price is unchanged.

If you forget Pareto efficiency

No one can be made better off without making someone else worse off.

If you forget Nash equilibrium

No player wants to change strategy alone.
