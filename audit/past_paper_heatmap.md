## Exam format observations

These observations are source-limited because the A3 paper itself is missing.

- **T/F section:** A1 and A2 source names show true/false and short-question memo material. A3 should therefore be prepared with high-risk true/false traps across the full semester, especially definitions, equilibrium conditions, graph interpretation, and distinction questions.
- **MCQ section:** A1 and A2 memo filenames explicitly include MCQs. The A3 warroom should include MCQ trap banks covering all included units.
- **Section C long/calculation questions:** A1 and A2 feedback filenames show written/long-question feedback. A3 should be built with long-answer templates, graph-drawing routines, calculations, and application paragraphs.
- **Negative marking:** Negative marking is known from the A2 preparation context, but exact A3 wording is not visible without A3 source files. The HTML should include a negative-marking caution until confirmed.
- **Calculation/graph emphasis:** A2 scope and the A2 MegaHub strongly emphasise graph, calculation, and application questions in Units 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, and 10. A3 adds Units 1, 2, and 3, so foundations must be integrated rather than treated as separate summaries.
- **Recurring traps:** The most dangerous likely traps are confusing Nash equilibrium with Pareto efficiency, dominant strategies with best responses, Pareto efficiency with fairness, monopoly price reading from MR instead of demand, legal tax burden with economic incidence, movement along a curve with a shift, public goods with government-provided goods, moral hazard with adverse selection, and Unit 6 minimum wage logic with Unit 8 competitive minimum wage logic.
