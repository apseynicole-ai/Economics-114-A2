## Exam format observations

- **Section A: True/False.** The extracted papers show T/F questions with negative marking. 2024 has 10 visible T/F questions in the official A3 paper; 2023 and 2022 also show 10 T/F questions each. Earlier embedded text also showed a 16-question A2-style section, but that is excluded from A3 counts.
- **Section B: MCQ.** The extracted A3 papers show MCQ sections with negative marking. 2024 has 20 MCQs; 2023 and 2022 have 15 MCQs each.
- **Section C: Descriptive/calculation questions.** Section C is the marks-heavy zone. It repeatedly tests calculations, graph interpretation, graph drawing, and applied economic reasoning.
- **Negative marking.** Section A uses `+1` and `-1/2`; Section B uses `+2` and `-1/4` in the extracted papers. The exam hub must coach selective answering and trap recognition.
- **Graph/calculation emphasis.** Repeated graph/calculation areas include MRS/MRT, payoff matrices, Angela-Bruno feasibility/bargaining, labour discipline/isocost curves, monopoly MR=MC, elasticity, competitive equilibrium, price controls, tax incidence, surplus/DWL, externalities and Pigouvian policy.
- **Repeated question styles.** A3 repeatedly asks students to distinguish close concepts, calculate from equations/tables, read graphs, draw policy graphs, and apply a model to a scenario.

## 80%+ implication

The first full content build should prioritise the exam warroom and markets hub because the real A3 papers repeatedly reward calculation accuracy, graph labelling, graph interpretation, and trap recognition. Unit 8 and Unit 7 should be built first, followed by Unit 10, Unit 3, Unit 6, Unit 4 and Unit 5. Unit 1 and Unit 2 still matter because they recur in T/F, MCQ and early Section C questions, especially GDP growth, capitalism, Malthus, isocosts, production and opportunity-cost logic.

A strong 80%+ system must therefore include: a negative-marking strategy, a formula bank, a graph redraw bank, worked calculation recipes, MCQ distractor explanations, T/F correction drills, and Section C answer templates. The final HTML should not only summarise topics; it must teach the exact repeated exam moves: identify the model, write the condition/formula, calculate cleanly, label the graph, explain the economic mechanism, and avoid the named trap.
